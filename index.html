    Get it running

    Install in four steps

    Watchdog is a direct download (an APK file) rather than a Play Store app, so you'll allow a one-time install from your browser. It takes about a minute.

    1

    Download the app

    On your Android phone, tap Download for Android below. You'll get a file named Watchdog.apk.

    It's about a large download, so Wi-Fi is a good idea.

    2

    Open the file

    Tap it in your download notification, or find it in Files › Downloads and tap to open.

    3

    Allow the install

    Android will ask to allow installs from your browser: tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, go back, then tap Install.

    If Play Protect warns about an unknown app, tap More details → Install anyway.

    4

    Open & allow alerts

    Launch Watchdog and allow location and notifications so it can measure distance and warn you.

    For on-time alerts, set its battery use to Unrestricted. What each permission is for

    What it watches

    One map for everything that threatens where you are

    Watchdog pulls from official feeds — wildfire agencies, USGS, the National Weather Service, and more — and puts them on a single map with distance and direction from you.

    Wildfires

    Active fires from national and state feeds plus satellite detections, with containment and perimeters drawn on the map.

    Proximity alerts

    A notification when a fire is within your radius — and again if it grows or containment changes, if you want.

    Earthquakes

    Recent USGS earthquakes worldwide, sortable by magnitude, distance, or time.

    Weather & red-flag alerts

    National Weather Service warnings — fire weather, wind, heat, flooding, winter, and more.

    Air quality

    Current AQI where you are, with plain-language guidance on what the number means.

    Volcanoes & outages

    Elevated volcanoes worldwide and large power outages near you, each toggleable.

    Support

    Getting the most out of Watchdog

    Common questions and fixes. If something here doesn't solve it, the developer reads every message — see Contact.

    Why does it need my location? ›
    Location is how Watchdog measures the distance and direction of a fire or hazard from you, and decides whether something is inside your alert radius. It's used on your device to sort and filter what you see and to time notifications. If you'd rather not share GPS, you can set a location manually in Settings → Location by city or ZIP.
    I'm not getting notifications ›
    Check three things: notifications are allowed for Watchdog in Android Settings → Apps → Watchdog → Notifications; your alert radius in the app is wide enough to include nearby activity; and battery optimization isn't stopping background refresh — set Watchdog to Unrestricted under battery settings. You can fire a test alert from Diagnostics → Send test notification.
    The map is blank or has no labels ›
    The map needs a network connection to load tiles. If it's blank, check connectivity, then pull up Diagnostics and tap Refresh now. Switching between satellite and street in the map controls forces a fresh style load.
    No fires are showing up ›
    Watchdog only shows fires the official feeds are currently reporting, so an empty list can be accurate. To rule out a data hiccup, open Diagnostics — it shows how many incidents loaded and when — and tap Refresh now. Data refreshes automatically about every 15 minutes.
    My location is wrong (I use a VPN) ›
    A VPN can throw off automatic location. Set your place explicitly in Settings → Location by typing a city and state or a ZIP code — that overrides GPS and keeps distances accurate.
    Does it drain my battery? ›
    Watchdog refreshes on a schedule (roughly every 15 minutes) rather than continuously, to keep battery use light. If Android has restricted it, background refreshes may be delayed — allowing unrestricted background activity gives the most timely alerts.
    What do the hazard tabs mean? ›
    Under Incidents, tabs appear for what you've enabled: Fires (with Active/Past), Quakes, Weather, AQI, and — when turned on in Settings — Volcanoes and Outages. Tapping a quake or weather alert jumps to it on the map; tapping a volcano opens its detail.
    Is my data private? ›
    Watchdog has no account and no login. Your location is used on your device to calculate distances and isn't part of any profile. The app talks to public data feeds (fire agencies, USGS, the National Weather Service, and similar) to fetch what to show you.

    Permissions, in plain terms

    Location
    Measures how far and which way a fire or hazard is from you, and whether it's inside your alert radius. Optional — you can set a location by hand instead.
    Notifications
    Lets Watchdog warn you when something enters your radius, and when a nearby fire grows or its containment changes.
    Background refresh
    Allows periodic checks (about every 15 minutes) so alerts arrive even when the app is closed. Governed by your battery settings.
